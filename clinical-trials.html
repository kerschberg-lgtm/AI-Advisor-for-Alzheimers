Clinical trials

Clinical trials in the United States and the European Union

The main prevention trials of the anti-amyloid drugs are fully enrolled, a second-generation antibody is now recruiting, and several smaller prevention studies are open. Here is what is active and how to search for a trial near you.

Updated 15 October 2026, 12:30 EDT

Trial status changes monthly. Every entry links to its registry record, which is the authoritative source. Eligibility must be confirmed with the study team, and any decision to join a trial should involve your own clinician.

Recruiting now Checked 15 October 2026

  • Phase 3USEU
    Trontinemab in cognitively unimpaired people at risk of progression to symptomatic Alzheimer's disease (PrevenTRON)
    Roche · NCT07717411 · sites in North and South America, Europe, Australia and Asia

    A prevention trial of a second-generation "brain-shuttle" anti-amyloid antibody in people with no symptoms but biomarker evidence of amyloid. Plans call for about 1,600 participants. Trontinemab is given less often than first-generation antibodies and has shown less ARIA (brain swelling) in earlier studies.

  • Phase 2US
    Obicetrapib in preclinical Alzheimer's disease
    NCT07794670 · United States

    Tests a cholesterol-lowering CETP inhibitor in people with preclinical Alzheimer's, a direct test of the heart-brain link described on the heart health page.

  • Phase 2US
    Sleep trial to prevent Alzheimer's disease (suvorexant)
    NCT04629547 · United States

    Tests whether the sleep medicine suvorexant, taken at night, lowers amyloid and tau in people at risk.

  • Phase 2US
    Alzheimer's Tau Platform: master protocol
    NCT06957418 · United States

    A platform trial testing several tau-directed treatments (including the AADvac1 vaccine) under one protocol.

  • Phase 2
    HT-4253 for the prevention of Alzheimer's disease in APOE4 carriers
    NCT07399171 · United Arab Emirates

    One of the few trials that specifically enrols APOE ε4 carriers. Listed here because 23andMe customers often ask; it is not in the US or EU.

  • Phase 2/3USEU
    DIAN-TU: remternetug in people at risk for, or with, inherited early-onset Alzheimer's
    Washington University · NCT05552157 and NCT06647498 · Americas, Europe, Australia

    For families with a known APP, PSEN1 or PSEN2 mutation. The 23andMe raw file can show some of these mutations, but a clinical test is required for enrolment.

  • ScreeningUSEU
    Screening study to determine eligibility for Alzheimer's disease trials
    NCT07177352 · multinational

    A blood-test screening study that routes eligible volunteers to treatment trials. A low-commitment way in for people who want to be considered.

Fully enrolled prevention trials (results pending)

  • Phase 3USEU
    AHEAD 3-45: lecanemab in preclinical Alzheimer's disease
    Eisai and NIA · NCT04468659 · enrolment completed October 2024 · primary completion estimated December 2028

    1,621 cognitively healthy adults with elevated brain amyloid, 73 percent of them APOE ε4 carriers, randomised to four years of lecanemab or placebo. Screening used a plasma p-tau217 test followed by PET.

  • Phase 3US
    TRAILBLAZER-ALZ 3: donanemab in preclinical Alzheimer's disease
    Eli Lilly · NCT05026866 · enrolment completed August 2024 · readout expected 2026 or 2027

    2,196 enrolled in the US and Japan; nearly half carry one APOE ε4 copy and 8 percent carry two. The trial ends when 350 participants show clinical worsening, so its date depends on how quickly people progress.

  • Phase 3US
    TRAILRUNNER-ALZ 3: remternetug in early Alzheimer's disease
    Eli Lilly · NCT06653153 · active, not recruiting

    A subcutaneous successor to donanemab in people with preclinical disease or mild cognitive impairment; screening used the plasma p-tau217 test.

Approved treatments and what they mean for trials

Lecanemab (Leqembi) and donanemab (Kisunla) are approved in the United States for early symptomatic Alzheimer's with confirmed amyloid, and lecanemab is approved in the European Union with restrictions for APOE ε4 homozygotes because of their higher ARIA risk. Both slow decline by roughly a quarter to a third over 18 months. Neither is approved for people without symptoms, which is exactly the question the prevention trials above are designed to answer. In 2026 the Alzheimer's pipeline held about 158 drugs in 192 trials, with growing numbers of tau-directed and anti-inflammatory agents.

Questions to ask before joining

  • What is the chance of receiving placebo, and is there an open-label extension afterwards?
  • What scans, lumbar punctures or infusions are required, and how often?
  • Will I learn my amyloid or APOE status, and is counselling provided?
  • What are the known side effects, and how are APOE ε4 carriers monitored for ARIA?
  • Can I take part if I have already received lecanemab or donanemab? Each sponsor decides individually.
